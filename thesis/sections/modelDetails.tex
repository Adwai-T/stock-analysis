\section{Gate Recurrent Unit (GRU) Models}

\FloatBarrier
\subsection{Introduction to GRU Architecture}

The Gated Recurrent Unit is a type of Recurrent Neural Network that is designed to process time series sequential data by retaining temporal patterns during training. While neural networks can produce impressive results by learning patterns, time series models such as GRU are specifically designed to consider sequential data, and maintain memory of the past that will then influence the future training.

GRU uses gating mechanism to determine how much historical information should be retained and what can be forgotten during the sequence processing. The following diagram shows the internal architecture of GRU.

\begin{figure}[h!]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{Images/methodology/gru.png} 
    \caption{Architecture of the GRU block.}
    \label{fig:GRU}
\end{figure}


There are two main or primary gates of GRU :

\begin{itemize}
  \item Update Gate – It controls how much of the past information is retained so it remains relevant for the next output.
  \item Reset Gate – It determines how much of the previous memory should be ignored while it integrates the new input signal.
\end{itemize}

This gated mechanism helps GRU avoid the common problem of vanishing gradient and exploding gradient commonly seen in the standard RNN and thus allows GRU to learn long term dependencies efficiently.

\FloatBarrier
\subsection{Mathematical Model for GRU}

\begin{align*}
    r_t &= \sigma(W_r x_t + U_r h_{t-1} + b_r) && \text{Reset Gate} \\
    z_t &= \sigma(W_z x_t + U_z h_{t-1} + b_z) && \text{Update Gate} \\
    \tilde{h}_t &= \tanh(W_h x_t + U_h (r_t \odot h_{t-1}) + b_h) && \text{Candidate Hidden State} \\
    h_t &= (1 - z_t) \odot h_{t-1} + z_t \odot \tilde{h}_t && \text{Final Hidden State}
\end{align*}

\subsection{Use of GRU in Project}

I wanted a baseline model that was better than RNNs but was simple, fast to train and could give good results with limited data available, such as training a model form a single companies data. GRU fulfils this role in spades. It generalises well on smaller dataset, but also learns patterns of that specific smaller dataset making it extremely capable. It also converges faster making training time smaller especially for the real-time nature of training that is required for the application. GRU gives perfect balance between model size, complexity and generalisation ability.

GRU also performs particularly well when trained with just the Raw features of stock data, Open High Low Close and Volume. Models trained as such produce stable and accurate predictions for that particular dataset. This also makes the model highly specific to that stock data, sacrificing generalisation for pattern learning and accuracy for that specific stock.

When GRU is trained on broader dataset such as NIFTY50 OR NIFTY200 the model definitely benefits from the broader dataset and variability, but it still lacks behind in performance as compared to models trained with LSTM, as LSTM is able to capture patterns better as it retains more of the past effecting the overall training.

\section{Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) Model}

\subsection{Overview of LSTM Architecture}

LSTM is considered one of the most popular extension of Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), which addresses the biggest issue with RNNs of vanishing and exploding gradient. It is designed to effectively learn patterns in time series data. Like GRU, LSTM achieves it through gated structures, specifically input gate, forget gate and output gate. These gated structures regulate how data flows forward through time and how past data is retained.

A new Memory state or Cell State is introduced in LSTM that stores useful past information across longer time span than RNNs and also GRU. This makes LSTM the choice for larger stock data, and training of models that generalise well and can learn historical patterns in financial time series forecasting. This makes them adaptable to short term gradual momentum shifts as well as long term historical patterns observed.

\subsection{Why Use LSTM in time series forecasting}

LSTM networks are capable of learning temporal dependencies very well and are thus used in time series forecasting application such as in this case Stock Market Forecasting. They are capable of learning longer patterns in data such as trend formations, and reversal patterns as well as handle non linear input.

In stock forecasting it is often said, History repeats itself, and the whole concept of Stock Forecasting based on Technical Analysis is based on this principle. LSTM models specialise in retaining information such as trends, oscillations and volatility cycle over time and are therefore one of the best choice in predicting next days closing price when the model is trained with sufficiently large dataset.

\FloatBarrier
\subsection{Mathematical Model for LSTM}

\begin{align*}
    f_t &= \sigma(W_f x_t + U_f h_{t-1} + b_f) && \text{Forget Gate} \\
    i_t &= \sigma(W_i x_t + U_i h_{t-1} + b_i) && \text{Input Gate} \\
    o_t &= \sigma(W_o x_t + U_o h_{t-1} + b_o) && \text{Output Gate} \\
    \tilde{c}_t &= \tanh(W_c x_t + U_c h_{t-1} + b_c) && \text{Candidate Cell State} \\
    c_t &= f_t \odot c_{t-1} + i_t \odot \tilde{c}_t && \text{Updated Cell State} \\
    h_t &= o_t \odot \tanh(c_t) && \text{Final Hidden State (Output)}
\end{align*}

\section{Transformer Model}

\subsection{Transformer Architecture}

Transformers were originally introduced in natural language processing for sequence to sequence learning tasks, where long range dependencies and contextual relationship capturing as essential. Transformer unlike traditional RNNs and their derivatives do not rely on sequential processing. They instead use self attention mechanism that allow them to evaluate the importance of different time steps relative to one another within the input sequence.

This learning behaviour of transformers allow them to weigh which portion of the historical sequence are most relevant to the prediction task at any given time. Transformers are also extremely computationally efficient as the attention is computed in parallel rather than iteratively. This makes them capable of scaling and modelling longer temporal patterns than RNNs or LSTM models that degrade the memory overtime.

They train better with larger data sets like all companies data and could be decent with NIFTY200.

  \item No recursive backpropagation

\subsection{Mathematical Model for Transformer Encoder}

\paragraph{Self-Attention Mechanism}

\begin{align*}
    Q &= X W_Q, \quad K = X W_K, \quad V = X W_V && \text{Linear Projections} \\
    A &= \text{softmax}\left(\frac{Q K^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) && \text{Attention Weights} \\
    \text{SA}(X) &= A V && \text{Self-Attention Output}
\end{align*}

Multi-head Self Attention with $H$ heads:

\begin{align*}
    \text{head}_h &= \text{SA}(X W_Q^{(h)}, X W_K^{(h)}, X W_V^{(h)}) \\
    \text{MHA}(X) &= \text{Concat}(\text{head}_1, \dots, \text{head}_H) W_O
\end{align*}

\paragraph{Transformer Encoder Layer}

\begin{align*}
    H'_1 &= \text{LayerNorm}\left(X + \text{MHA}(X)\right) && \text{Residual + Norm after MHA} \\
    \text{FFN}(H'_1) &= \phi(H'_1 W_1 + b_1) W_2 + b_2 && \text{Position-wise Feed-Forward} \\
    H_1 &= \text{LayerNorm}\left(H'_1 + \text{FFN}(H'_1)\right) && \text{Final Encoder Output for Layer}
\end{align*}

$\phi(\cdot)$ - ReLU or GELU activation.

\section{Conv1D + LSTM Hybrid Model}

\subsection{Introduction to the Hybrid Architecture}

I wanted to combine the benefits of LSTM with the strengths of Convolutional neural networks (CNN) in time series forecasting.

The 1D CNN layer acts as a localized feature extrator similar to how humans try to find patterns in the candle stick data, such as hammer patterns, Doji, Inverted Hammer, Shootin Star etc. The CNN output can then be passed to LSTM that would convert them into temporal patterns.

\subsection{Motivation for Using a Hybrid Model in Time-Series Forecasting}

This hybrid design helps address:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Capture local stock pattern with CNN
  \item Rapid fluctuation addressed
  \item Local trend dampened
  \item Smooth out features before they can be sent to LSTM
\end{itemize}
