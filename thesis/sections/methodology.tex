\section{Data Preprocessing}

Preprocessing data is an important step in training a model as it determines the accuracy of the trained model. The data should be consistent without outliers as they could skew the overall results. Similarly missing data should also be handled.

\subsection{Handling Missing Values}

Holidays when stock markets remain closed do not produce any data and is automatically handled by the Kite Connect API as it skips those days from the data it sends. As while preparing data the dates are only considered to maintain the series, it does not need to be handled separately.

Missing Data issue is mostly encountered for smaller companies that do not consistently train and can have skewed data for the days when there is huge movement seen in the stocks. These stocks are completely skipped during training for the models trained on All stock data.

\subsubsection{Handling Non-Trading Days}

Market does not operate on Saturday and Sundays as well as on public holidays. Thus we have missing data for those days.

Forward feeding could be used in this case as that does not effect or impact the temporal properties and pattern of the data.

\begin{equation}
x_t =
\begin{cases}
x_{t}, & \text{if value exists} \\
x_{t-1}, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Missing Volume Replacement}

There were rare instance where the volume data was missing. Volume is one of the most important feature in the training the model to understand the market dynamics as it reflects market participation and thus liquidity. Removing or replacing the value with forward filling could impact overall behaviour that was suppose to be learnt by the model.

The missing volume values were replaced with 7 days rolling average that takes into consideration a larger span of time than just forward filling.

\begin{equation}
Volume_t = \frac{1}{7} \sum_{i=1}^{7} Volume_{t-i}
\end{equation}

This method I found was much better than removing data, or replacing with complete data average or repeating prior values, in preserving the overall context. Thus preventing sudden change that could be introduced by other methods, but also preserves the overall movement that is expected for that time.

\subsubsection{Missing Indicator Rows}

Some Indicators are calculated considering the past few days data such as Moving Average, or Volatility estimators. Thus they could need a minimum amount of past data to calculate for the set window. In Most cases the values are interpolated or could be entirely skipped.

\begin{equation}
D_{clean} = \{x \in D \mid \text{all features at } t \text{ are non-null}\}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Rational for Missing Data Strategy}

Using different approaches for different data like missing base features, skipping companies with extreme volatility, rolling average for volume data ensures that the essence of the market behaviour can be learned by the model.

Following were the points considered while selecting the techniques used to overcome missing data.

\begin{itemize}
    \item Help preserve historical trends
    \item Prevent artificially creating volatility or distortion in data.
    \item As using Time series model, ensure that the sequential nature of the data is maintained.
\end{itemize}

While training any machine learning model, and especially for time series model where sequential data is used there is a huge impact of data preprocessing on model accuracy and training convergence.

\subsection{Scaling and Normalization}

While training with multiple it is important that the data is normalised as the price can vary hugely between companies. The goal of training a time series model on for stock is to capture the  different pattern and use this pattern to predict the next days closing. Normalisation ensures that the specific companies price are not considered, just the movement of price from day to day is considered.

\begin{equation}
x_{\text{scaled}} = \frac{x - \min(x)}{\max(x) - \min(x)}
\end{equation}

During inference the min max of the that companies data is considered. Thus once the normalised result are inferred, that min max can then be used to calculate the scaled closing value.

Simple Min Max scaling is used to Normalise data in this project. The following formula is used for the time dependant features.

\begin{equation}
x' = \frac{x - x_{\text{min}}}{x_{\text{max}} - x_{\text{min}}}
\end{equation}

\section{Feature Engineering}

I in this project have tried to compare the effectiveness of Feature Engineering vs the Raw Stock data. Thus feature engineering is a major part of this project.

Raw stock data considers valuable information, it can be prominently see in candle stick data, which has been used since a long time and is one of the foundational Technical analysis tools used. But for a machine learning model, especially those considering time series data, it could be helpful to consider other technical analysis indicators which could enhance the existing patterns in the data.

In this project I have tried to consider the combinations of Technical indicators that consider historical pricing and volume together so both are given equal importance. Technical Indicators transform raw values into structured input that enhance the pattern learning and thus improve the predictive performance of the model.

\subsection{Raw Feature Selection}

The raw data retrieved from Kite Connect API for each company contains the Open, High, Low, Close and Volume information for each day. These form the base features of the stock data that is used to train the models.

The feature set is defined as:

\begin{equation}
X_t = \{Open_t, High_t, Low_t, Close_t, Volume_t\}
\end{equation}

The consistency of base features between companies provides a solid base on which additional features can be calculated.

\subsection{Windowing Strategy}

As all the models trained in this project are time series models such as LSTM, GRU, Transformers and CNN-LSTM, it is important to maintain consistency between the windowing strategy that will work for all models so the results can be effectively compared.

\begin{equation}
X = [X_{t-n}, X_{t-n+1}, ..., X_{t-1}] \rightarrow y = Close_t
\end{equation}

\begin{align*}
    X &= [\text{features for days } t-19 \text{ to } t] \\
    y &= \text{closing price at day } t+1
\end{align*}

Sliding window strategy is chosen for dividing data into sets. The window size of 20 is used throughout the project to maintain consistency and also maintain the scope of the project. I found that this window size was large enough that the model could observe short term trends and volatility behaviour but is not computationally as intensive due to the limitation of my hardware used in this project.

\subsection{Training Test Validation Structuring}

Dividing data into Training and Test is an important final step in feature engineering. It is important that the training as well as test data maintains the temporal order. Unlike other machine learning model training and testing where the data might be shuffled, time series model require that the data preserves its order both while it is used for training and then while testing.

Data is divided as follows :

\begin{equation}
D = D_{\text{train}} \cup D_{\text{test}}, \quad \text{with } t_{\text{train}} < t_{\text{test}}
\end{equation}

Validation method uses time series split without any shuffling.

The callback method is used for early stopping and learning rate reduction. After 6 epochs of training without improvement in validation error, the training is stopped early so compute could be saved and training time could be reduced.

Loss curve and predictions are generated after training using training data set. This is an important visual representation in comparing the models.

\subsection{Indices Used}

Stock indices from NSE and BSE have been used as solid reflection of the overall health of market. Segment indices as well as the top stock are maintained by both BSE and NSE. In this project I have used the indices from NSE like NIFTY50, NIFTY100 and NIFTY200 as they give the best combination of the stable bigger companies of India in NIFTY50 and bit more diverse companies in NIFTY200.

\section{Technical Indicators}

Technical Indicators are mathematical transformation of raw features of historical price and volume data that are used to enhance and extract meaningful market patterns and thus help models learn the patterns better. Raw data can be combined to reveal information such as volatility, momentum, or even market sentiment which is not always directly visible or interpretable from raw features.

In this project I have tried to use the most widely used Technical Indicators that will help capture the trend, momentum, volatility and volume. The following sections I will discuss in detail the Technical indicators used and the purpose they serve in combination with other indicators. I will also discuss the advantages and disadvantages and if there are ways to mitigate the limitations while using them.

\subsection{Exponential Moving Average (EMA)}

The Exponential moving average is a derivative of Simple moving average that gives more weight to recent price values, thus making it more responsive to recent movement. Both Simple Moving Average and Exponential Moving average fall in the same category that they are used as trend indicators. Thus giving additional weight-age to recent data EMA reacts better and follows trends more closely than SMA.

Exponential Moving Average is defined by the following formula.

\begin{equation}
EMA_t = \alpha \cdot P_t + (1 - \alpha) \cdot EMA_{t-1}
\end{equation}

where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $P_t$ is the closing price at time $t$
    \item $\alpha = \frac{2}{n+1}$ is the smoothing factor
    \item $n$ is the lookback period (12 or 26 in this study)
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Advantages:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Main advantage of this indicator is that it reacts faster to the trend changes than Simple moving average.
    \item It helps indicate the market direction and momentum.
    \item It can be used to enhance trends while being used for training.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Limitations:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item As it reacts to recent changes more than SMA, it might introduce noise during high market volatility.
    \item Is very market dependent and needs to have the window to be decided accordingly
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Role in This Project:}  

In this project I have choosen EMA as the trend indicator. It helps smooth out the directional patterns ompared to just raw price squence that could vary wildly on some days. This is highly effective when considering large datasets.

The soothing of these irregularities also help with better learning of the model as it complements how models like LSTM and CNN-LSTM work underneath.

\subsection{Relative Strength Index (RSI)}

The Relative Strength Index (RSI) is traditioanlly used to understand if a stock is overbought or oversold.

RSI indicates the price momentum, meaning the speed at which the price changes. This can be an effective indicator of petential buying or selling signal.

The RSI is calculated as:

\begin{equation}
RS = \frac{\text{Average Gain}}{\text{Average Loss}}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
RSI = 100 - \left( \frac{100}{1 + RS} \right)
\end{equation}

\textbf{Advantages:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Captures short-term momentum
    \item Indicate reversal behavior
    \item Detecting buying or selling pressure
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Limitations:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item They can be susceptible to false indicators especially in trend driven markets.
    \item Extremely sensitive to short term volatility.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Role in This Project:}  

RSI are used as a counterbalance to the trend heavy indicators such as EMA. It bring oscillatory behavior to the overall learning.

It is very effective when considering larger data sets, but can fail especially in saller data sets such as when considering single company data. In those small data sets the RAW features already contain enough momentum information.

\subsection{Average True Range (ATR)}

The Average True Range (ATR) is a volatility indicator.

It is designed to measure the degree of price range expansion or contraction over time.

\begin{equation}
TR_t = \max \left(
\begin{array}{l}
H_t - L_t, \\
|H_t - C_{t-1}|, \\
|L_t - C_{t-1}|
\end{array}
\right)
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
ATR_t = EMA(TR_t, 14)
\end{equation}

\textbf{Advantages:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Quantify market volatility
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Limitations:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Price direction is not represented.
    \item Could emplify noise, especially in smaller dataset.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Role in This Project:}  

ATR is used to indicate volatility dynamic to the feature set. The indicator was especially useful when used with larger datasets. It was detremental to the training when used with smaller dataset as it could introducte a lot of noise.

\subsection{On-Balance Volume (OBV)}

On-Balance Volume (OBV) is a volume-based indicator that can be used to track buying and selling pressure.

\begin{equation}
OBV_t = 
\begin{cases}
OBV_{t-1} + V_t, & \text{if } C_t > C_{t-1} \\
OBV_{t-1} - V_t, & \text{if } C_t < C_{t-1} \\
OBV_{t-1}, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

$V_t$ - traded volume.

\textbf{Advantages:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Volume shifts preceding price movement
    \item Helps reveal accumulation or distribution patterns
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Limitations:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Is very Sensitive to volume spikes
    \item Does not work well with low liquidity stocks
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Summary of Indicator Contribution}

Indicator based features are used in training, both in larger as well as smaller models. The single stock data do not benifit as much from the stock indicators, but multi company models trained with features that include the Indicators show significanlty better results. This is beause in larger datasets they enhance the existing patterns, that they represent.
