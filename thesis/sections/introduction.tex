 \section{Introduction and Background}

The first stock exchange was the Amsterdam stock exchange, that was established in 1602 by the Dutch East India company to trade stocks. Since the foundation, stock exchanges have been consistently proven as easy way for people to participate in the economy, and get involved with businesses. This has also helped business raise capital further bringing economic prosperity and creating jobs as well as wealth for investors. It has also helped with innovation as it has helped companies smaller as well as larger to raise money for their new projects, distributing risks and making capital available for development of new products and research.

India's first stock exchange was the Bombay stock exchange (BSE) started in 1875 and is also Asia's oldest stock exchange. Similar to the other stock exchanges in the world the Indian stock market has brought economic prosperity and opportunity for people to invest in the development of Indian companies. It has also help create opportunity for foreign investors to invest in India that has greatly helped Indian companies raise capital and develop new and innovative products for India. There are a number of Stock exchanges in India, but Bombay stock exchange (BSE) and National Stock Exchange (NSE) are the two main and the most important stock exchanges on which most of the companies whose stocks are tradable in India are listed.

\section{Evolution of Technical Analysis}

The exchanges in the past use to maintain physical ledgers of pricing action in the market, and trades used to be done using verbal communication and hand gestures in an open outcry system to execute trades. Then Manual record keeping was standard.
The development of stock tickers in 1860s followed by electronic system in 1960s were major milestones in how the stock market operated and how the users could participate and analyse stocks for trading. The creation of fully electronic exchange like NASDAQ in 1971 opened doors to collection, storage and access of huge amount of stock data that could be used to analyse price action over time. This also meant that it opened doors to analysis of different events further deepening the understanding and development of techniques for forecasting prices.  The internet further helps in automation of data flow and improved accessibility as well as improved data quality as other participants could also collect and store data in even more granular detail.

With the emergence of Internet trading and availability of huge past data, gave rise to increased competition and thus research both in Fundamental and Technical analysis. Technical Analysis overtime developed in to an extremely completive, structured and quantitative practice based on mathematical models.
The first formalized technical analysis pattern is considered the candlestick technique developed by the Japanese rice merchants Munehisa Homma in the 18th century. The candle stick pattern help tracks and better visualize the open, low, high, close of the time scale decided and also helps recognize emergent patterns formed as a result of plotting the candle stick overtime. These patterns were used then to understand market movement and predict the price action.

Further competitiveness of markets and rise of internet trading, helped develop technical indicators including Simple Moving Average (SMA), Volume based Trend lines, Chart patterns based on Candle stick data, Moving Average Convergence Divergence (MACD), Relative Strength Index (RSI) and are still some of the most popular indicators used in trading today. These indicators helped traders better visualize and understand the impact of different event on price when working with historical data proving that Technical analysis could give meaningful information and thus aid in forecasting prices in the future.

As Technical Analysis is heavily dependent on data and is basically based on developing mathematical model to understand performance of market and forecasting future to maximize probability of good predictions to make profits, use machine learning models became the obvious way forward. AI models are extremely good at adapting mathematical models and are devoid of human biases. They also have good scalability as compared to the number of humans that will be needed to study and interpret data.


\section{Limitations of Classical Forecasting Models}

Technical analysis before Machine Learning method were introduced heavily relied on mathematical and statistical model of time series data such as Moving Average, Oscillators such as RSI and stochastic oscillators, Moving Average Convergence Divergence, Volume Indicators which are effective at identifying baseline patterns. They assume linearity and stationarity of properties which mostly does not match the real work behaviour of the market.

Market data is inherently noisy, nonlinear, non-stationary and chaotic. The market conditions can shift rapidly due to external triggers may it be with news related to the specific company itself or it could be related to larger factors like political or global factors. Classical technical pattern fails to recognise and adapt to these external factors that could have huge movement not seen in historical data.

The prime example of failure of Technical analysis could be seen during the economic downturn during the Covid Pandemic during the period of 2020-2021. This also extends to the rapid recovery of the market in the later years not following the linear grown that the market followed before. 

The main reason for the failure of Technical analysis is it treats time series data in isolation rather than larger context, may it be the Fundamentals or the Sentiment changes. Machine learning could solve the issue by creating a model that combines all the three factors Fundamental, sentimental and Technical indicators. In this project though I focus mainly on use of Machine learning to create Technical analysis models as combining all three would create too big of a scope to focus on and explore in detail the impact of Technical indicators and their effectiveness when used to train a machine learning model.
 
\section{Machine Learning as a Solution}

Machine learning models are excellent at learning relationships from data, and can better accommodate for complex non linear relationships between features. The classical Technical analysis methods rely heavily on linearity of relationship and human intuition to understand these when there are deviations. Thus, machine learning models trained on the huge amount of data now available for the stock can generalize understanding the underlying patterns that human interpreters could miss.

Human interpretation of stock mathematical models has the limitation that they can consider only a limited about of feature and indicators in combination while making a forecasting prediction making the prediction heavily biased by the signals provided by those particular indicators. As all Technical indicators have their limitations and combining them could elevate the biases that these limitations might bring, machine learning models become an obvious solution. 

Machine learning models, especially deep learning models can consider a huge number of features while training and can also combine different Technical indicators together, thus reducing the impact of each individual Technical indicators. They also can interpret and learn complex relationships between these indicators and their overall impact on the forecasting. Humans on the other hand could miss, ignore or have biases towards considering these indicators.

Traditional machine learning algorithms saw good success in forecasting as Technical analysis was hugely based on mathematical modelling of stock data. Algorithms such as Support Vector machines, Decision Tree, Random Forests, Logistical Regression and even Linear regression gave impressive result while forecasting. Taking the example of SVM models when combined with Technical indicators like Moving Average, Bollinger Bands, RSI, Stochastic Oscillators etc (Indicators help in understand different features of market like price action and volume of trade) gave impressive result achieving up to 77.8\% of accuracy, and also demonstrated robustness especially for volatile stocks.

Introduction of neural networks and time series neural networks such as GRU and LSTM gave even better forecasting results. Deep learning used in Technical analysis are even better at forming complex relationship and combining patterns to give very accurate forecasting. They are also capable of including factors like Fundamentals of the company and events like seasonal ness of products sold or announcement of earnings. Even further inclusion of LLM to  has enabled the inclusion of sentimental data, closely tracking news and social media to understand and include that factor in the larger models.

Thus, Machine learning has made it possible to integrate Technical, Fundamental and Sentimental data in an automated system, that can consider a huge number of features to effectively predict and forecast prices. 

\section{Importance of Technical Indicators}

The raw data that is collected mostly consist of data that is required to create candlestick charts, as it is the most popular form of charts used in Technical Analysis. This data consists of open, close, low, high and also includes the volume of trade. These data features then can further used to calculate Technical Indicators that combine them is a way that creates distinctive feature.

Indicators such as MACD, RSI, and EMA provide diverse patterns, momentum, surges, trend strength, and volatility that enhance the data that the Machine learning algorithms can interpret and form stronger relationship between features. Studies have shown that combining these indicators that target a particular signal each provide better data for algorithm making the algorithm generalize better.

These indicators also reduce noise and fluctuation that might have been caused due to temporary instability in market. Making the supervised learning models and deep learning neural networks trained on this time series data more stable and generalized.

\section{Identified Research Gap}

While existing research demonstrates significant advancements in stock prediction using ML, deep learning, and hybrid models, several gaps remain. First, a large portion of the work focuses on markets such as the NASDAQ, NYSE, and Shanghai Stock Exchange, with far less emphasis on emerging markets such as India. This creates generalization concerns, as financial behavior differs across regions due to regulation, liquidity conditions, economic stability, and trading culture.

Second, many studies prioritize prediction accuracy as the primary evaluation measure. However, high classification accuracy does not always translate to profitable or low-risk trading outcomes, especially when ignoring transaction fees, slippage, market volatility, or drawdown effects. Recent papers highlight that success metrics should incorporate profitability measures such as return on investment (ROI), maximum drawdown, and the Sharpe ratio.

Third, deep learning approaches, although powerful, are often criticized for their black-box nature, making them difficult to interpret and validate. 

This lack of transparency poses challenges in regulated financial environments where decision justification is required.

Finally, while hybrid models show promise, there is limited research on systematically comparing classical ML, deep learning, and hybrid approaches specifically combined with technical analysis inputs under consistent evaluation frameworks  especially using Indian market data.

These gaps collectively indicate a need for structured research that evaluates machine learning-driven technical analysis within the context of Indian market behavior while incorporating explainability and meaningful financial evaluation metrics.

\section{Problem Statement}

Based on the identified research gap, the key challenges addressed in this thesis are, traditional stock forecasting methods and standalone technical analysis approaches are limited in their ability to model nonlinear, volatile, and complex stock price behavior, particularly in emerging markets such as India. While machine learning and deep learning approaches have shown promising results, there is insufficient structured evaluation of their effectiveness when technical indicators are used as input features, especially in comparison across multiple model families and under realistic trading evaluation criteria.

This study seeks to address the problem by evaluating machine learning models for stock forecasting using technical indicators as engineered inputs, assessing their predictive capability, interpretability, and practical applicability.

\section{Research Objectives}

The main objective of this thesis is to evaluate the performance of machine learning models applied to stock technical analysis and determine their suitability for practical forecasting and decision-making.

This objective expands into the following sub-objectives:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item To collect, preprocess, and structure historical stock data suitable for machine learning and deep learning models.
    \item To compute and integrate a diverse set of technical indicators as engineered feature representations.
    \item To train and evaluate multiple machine learning algorithms, including classical ML models and neural architectures.
    \item To compare model performance using both statistical accuracy metrics and financially meaningful performance metrics.
    \item To interpret and analyze model behavior to assess generalization capability and practical feasibility in real-world markets.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Research Questions}

Aligned with the objectives, the study aims to answer the following questions:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Do machine learning models improve prediction performance when combined with technical analysis indicators compared to raw prices?
    \item Which category of model  classical machine learning, deep learning, or hybrid  performs best under consistent evaluation?
    \item Can models trained on historical pricing patterns generalize to unseen market data, particularly in volatile environments?
    \item Do statistical accuracy metrics correlate reliably with trading performance metrics such as Sharpe ratio or return on investment (ROI)?
    \item Are models based on technical analysis suitable for deployment in emerging markets such as India, or do market conditions reduce their effectiveness?
\end{enumerate}

Answering these questions provides measurable insight into the viability and limitations of applying machine learning to technical stock forecasting.

\section{Scope, Limitations, and Assumptions}

The scope of this research is restricted to:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Historical stock price data
  \item Daily time-series resolution
  \item Models trained on select high-volume stocks
  \item Forecast horizon limited to short-term directional or price movement prediction
\end{enumerate}

Key limitations include:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Results may vary across different stocks, time horizons, and economic conditions.
  \item The study excludes macroeconomic indicators, textual sentiment, or high-frequency tick data.
  \item Interpretability challenges may persist for deeper neural architectures.
  \item Model performance may degrade during extreme market volatility or structural regime shifts.
  \item Assumptions include the availability of consistent market data, stable economic conditions during evaluation, and reproducibility of indicator formulas across software implementations.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Novelty and Contributions}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The contributions of this research are summarized as follows:
  \item A structured comparative study of classical ML, deep learning, and hybrid models using a uniform technical analysis–based dataset.
  \item Inclusion of both accuracy metrics and financial performance metrics, aligning evaluation with real-world trading relevance.
  \item Application of machine learning-driven technical analysis specifically to Indian market conditions, addressing a documented research gap. 
  \item Development of a replicable preprocessing and modeling pipeline for future applied research and industry use.
\end{enumerate}

These contributions offer practical value for quantitative researchers, retail investors, academic researchers, and financial institutions.
