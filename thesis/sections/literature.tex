\section{Stock Market Forecasting and Technical Analysis}

\subsection{Introduction}

Before the advent of machines and computers for visualization the technical analysis of stocks was limited to rule based trading systems derived from classical economic forecasting. Later with the rise of computer age and availability of data visualization trades started to rely on visual interpretation of price patterns and trends.  Signals and moving averages to make investment decisions.

Brock, Lakonishok, and LeBaron (1992) [1], decisions provided the first systematic evaluation of technical analysis that demonstrated that rules such as Moving Average crossovers had statistical significance and could give significant returns in right market conditions. Rule Based forecasting [2]. is however limited by chaotic, non linear and non stationary nature of Stock markets. As time and technology progressed the research in technical analysis moved from rule based systems to more computational modelling to improve predictive stability and reduce human error and bias. This also increased the scope of Technical Analysis by including different indicators for volatility, momentum and behavioural signals. In the following Literature Review I will try to show the gradual shift from traditional methods the modern techniques using machine learning and deep learning for market forecasting. [3] [4]

\subsection{Classical Forecasting Techniques}

Older or classical Forecasting of time series data relied on deterministic and statistical framework such as Simple Moving Average, or Auto Regressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA). Moving averages. They severed as the baseline tool for detecting a trend and smoothing it for better understanding and visualization. But these indicators are limited by lag and inability to adjust to extreme volatility. [1]

ARIMA, introduced by Box and Jenkins (1970) [5], is a systematic approach to time series modelling that takes into consideration autoregression, differencing and noise smoothing. ARIMA assumes stationarity and linear structure conditions, which is rarely the case with stock markets. [6] There were improvements to ARIMA such as Seasonal ARIMA and volatility aware extension like GARCH that further improved the predictions that could be made but still struggled to accurately capture the market movement. [7]

In the case of Long term horizon forecasting Holt Winter method was significantly more successful in modelling trends and seasonality. Still it had it limitations during rapid spikes in trends, or reversals, or significant movement driven spikes.[8]. These limitations lead to shift towards approaches that could capture nonlinear and adaptive Realtime dynamic dependencies.

\subsection{Traditional Trading Strategies}

Research by Murphy(1999) [9] and Achelis (2001) [10] brought indicators taxonomies and classified them into Trend Following like Simple Moving Average (SMA), Exponential Moving Average (EMA),  Moving Average Convergence Divergence (MACD), Momentum based (RSI, Stochastic Oscillators, Williams \%R), Volatility based (ART, Bollinger Bands), and volume based (OBV) categories.

Brock et al. (1992) [1], shows that moving average breakout and support resistance rules generate abnormal returns when compared with random walk baseline. Similarly Neely et al. (2014) [11], has shown that RSI and MACD exhibit validity in market and repeatedly out performed as compared to random walk hypothesis assumptions [3].
 
Park and Irwin's meta-study (2007) [12] established that multi-indicators system could consistently outperform any single rule strategy. This was especially true in volatile environments.

\subsection{Traditional Machine Learning models in Forecasting}

Traditional machine learning techniques started to be used for forecasting with the advent of computer age and availability of powerful hardware to train these models. Early machine learning models addressed the issue of bridging the gap between rule based trading and structural statistical forecasting.

Support Vector Machine (SVM), Random Forest (RF), Decision Tree (DT) and Naïve Bayes formed the foundation of the machine learning models during this time. SVM was used in directional prediction and showed good results when trained with technical indicators. Combining Bollinger Band, moving average, stochastic Oscillator, RSI and Aroon Oscillator gave an accuracy of 77.8\% on volatile dataset. [13]. Krenel-based SVM are still used frequently due to their ability to model non linear boundaries, even with smaller datasets.

Similarly Decision tree based models were traditionally used to due to their interpretability that is very important in stock market as money is involved. Studies have also applied multiple indicators to NSE equities reporting an accuracy upwards of 80\%, showing that simpler models can outperform complex models when used with indicators in certain environments. [14].

\section{AI Driven Stock Forecasting}

As the technology and especially the advent of extremely good computers with very high compulational ability, there was use of Machine leanring in every field. Stock forecasting was no different with infact one of the most profit driven use of Machine leaarning. And hence a lot of bit financal institues became interested in the use of ML. They worked on diverse datasets and including as many features and aspects of the financial space.

Machine learning studies such as Huang et al. \cite{Huang2022PredictiveModeling} demonstrated that supervised learning techniques could capture price dynamics more effectively than rule-based or traditional indicators based models. Even so they were often limited by feature dependence and also poor generalization. They are also extremely sensitive to non-stationary behavior.


Introduction of RNN was already a huge leap forward in creating model that need to consider temporal data. Hochreiter and Schmidhuber's foundational Long Short-Term Memory model \cite{Hochreiter1997LSTM} made it possible to retain long-term dependencies in sequential data, and also addressed the vanishing gradient limitations that is very common in RNNs.

Cho et al. \cite{Cho2014GRU} introduced the Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU), created a simplified gating mechanism while retaining temporal memory properties. GRUs are extremely efficient and hence became immediate favourite in financial forecasting. It also helps that they can generalize and converge well with limited amount of data as compared to LSTM.

This trend continues with Smarte and Ho \cite{Smarte2021ReviewOfMachineLearning} and Zhu \cite{Zhu2025StockPredictionReview}, who showed that RNNs remain one of the core part of financial forecasting and stock analysis.

Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), introduced by LeCun et al. \cite{LeCun1998CNN}, used for feature extraction expectailly in structure domain.Jin \cite{Jin2024CNNStockMarket} included CNNs in the stock forcasting and stock data analysis.

Vaswani et al. \cite{Vaswani2017Attention} added Transformers to the mix of algorithms used in training forecasting models. The success of transformer in the natural language processing meant that they could be included in sentimental analysis for predicting stock prices based on the overall market temprament based on news as well as social media \cite{Khanpuri2024SentimentAnalysis}, multimodal forecasting \cite{Huang2025DeepLearning}, and LLM-driven trading pipelines \cite{Cao2024LLMStock}. Cilingiroglu \cite{Cilingiroglu2025SentimentChangingPrice} used transformers to create models for inter industrial patter recognition.

\section{Evaluation Challenges, Research Gaps and Inconsistencies}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Data consistency and ways to deal with data inconsistencies when it comes to stock data
    \item Overfitting.
    \item Limited generalizability.
    \item Calrity and explanability of models
    \item Indian stock exchanges remain unexplored for forecasting behavious.
    \item Inconsistency in market trends caused due to India specific events such as government policy, overall impact of imports, and depends on other markets.
    \item Different Indian industries are dependent on different factors that set their price trends. Capturing them is difficult as they are not consistent with each other.
    \item Different industries in india is also dependent on different gio political regions and thus unstability in these regions causes different inpact on the trends experienced by the overall market.
    \item Service industies remain one of the biggest contributers in the Indian stock market and have their own challanges.
\end{itemize}
